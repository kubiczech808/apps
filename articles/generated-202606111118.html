Why flat dollar cost averaging might be diluting your stack

When we talk about dollar cost averaging, we usually focus entirely on price. We buy more when the price is low and less when it's high. But price is a lagging indicator of sentiment. Hashrate, on the other hand, represents real-world capital expenditure. It is the physical energy and hardware committed to securing the blockchain.

If the hashrate doubles, it means the network is twice as secure, competition is fiercer, and the underlying asset is fundamentally more robust. If your monthly investment stays flat while the hashrate goes vertical, your purchasing power is essentially diluting relative to the growth of the network's infrastructure. You are contributing the same amount of fiat to a system that has grown exponentially more secure and resource-intensive.

How the Bitcoin DCA velocity multiplier: Scaling your accumulation based on network hashrate growth actually works

The core idea behind the bitcoin dca velocity multiplier: scaling your accumulation based on network hashrate growth is simple. You establish a baseline DCA amount and a baseline hashrate. As the hashrate increases, you scale up your DCA amount proportionally using a multiplier.

For example, if your baseline buy is $50 a week at a hashrate of 500 EH/s, and the hashrate climbs to 600 EH/s, your multiplier becomes 1.2. Your new weekly buy automatically adjusts to $60.

When you implement the bitcoin dca velocity multiplier: scaling your accumulation based on network hashrate growth, you are essentially matching your capital allocation to the security budget of the network. You are buying more aggressively when the fundamental network security is at its strongest.

Obviously, hashrate can fluctuate wildly week-to-week due to variance in block discovery, so I don't recommend adjusting your buys daily. I prefer looking at the 30-day moving average of the hashrate and updating my multiplier quarterly. This keeps things smooth and prevents short-term mining variance from messing with my budget.
