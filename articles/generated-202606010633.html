The game-changing shift in corporate crypto accounting

Historically, the biggest roadblock for any small or medium business wanting to hold Bitcoin was the archaic accounting rules. Under the old rules, Bitcoin was treated as an intangible asset. If the price dropped, you had to write down the value on your books as an impairment loss. But if the price went up, you couldn't record those gains until you actually sold. It was a lose-lose situation that made company balance sheets look artificially weak.

Thankfully, the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) finally updated these rules to fair-value accounting. Now, businesses can report Bitcoin at its current market value. This means your balance sheet actually reflects reality, making it much more attractive for small businesses to hold Bitcoin as a treasury asset.
