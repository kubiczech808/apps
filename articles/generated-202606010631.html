Understanding why your static 100 dollar DCA is shrinking and how to inflation-proof your accumulation

The trap here is the comfort of automation. We set up an exchange integration, forget about it, and feel good because we are "doing the work." But life moves forward, and if your income grows while your DCA stays flat, you are actually becoming less invested in your future. I learned this the hard way when I realized that, in real terms, I was actually buying less Bitcoin than I was two years ago.

To fix this, I had to stop treating my DCA as a "set and forget" chore. I started adjusting my buy amount by a small percentage every time I got a raise or even when I managed to cut a recurring subscription I didn't need. It’s not about finding massive amounts of extra cash; it’s about aligning your investment growth with your life growth. Moving beyond fixed amounts

Most mainstream financial advice tells you to pick a number and stick to it until you retire. I think that is a mistake. If your salary increases by 5% but your investment stays flat, you are losing. I’ve started treating my Bitcoin buys like a tax on my own lifestyle. When I get a bonus, a portion goes straight into my stack.

I know some people get nervous about automating more money into an asset that fluctuates so wildly. That’s why I built a tool to automate my DCA buys directly through API keys on exchanges like Binance. It takes the emotion out of it. If the price is high, I buy a little less; if it’s low, I get more. But the key is that the dollar amount grows with my capacity to earn.

Obviously, I am not your financial advisor, and you should definitely do your own research before moving your money around. Crypto is volatile, and you should only invest what you are comfortable losing. I personally keep most of my holdings on a Trezor hardware wallet because I don't like leaving funds on exchanges once the purchase is made.

The inflation factor

The reason why your static 100 dollar dca is shrinking and how to inflation-proof your accumulation ultimately comes down to acknowledging that fiat currency is a leaky bucket. When you hold dollars, you are holding a depreciating asset. When you trade them for Bitcoin, you are opting out of that system.

If you aren't increasing your DCA, you are effectively accepting that your wealth will be diluted by the same monetary expansion that makes your $100 buy less today than it did yesterday. It’s a subtle shift in mindset, but it’s the difference between just "buying crypto" and actually building a long-term position that survives the inevitable debasement of the dollar.

I’ve made plenty of mistakes, like trying to time the market during the last cycle instead of just keeping my accumulation steady. I almost stopped my DCA entirely when the price dipped, which would have been a disaster for my average cost. Now, I just let the automation handle the heavy lifting. It’s much easier to sleep at night when you know your strategy is designed to scale with your life, rather than being stuck in a static loop that loses its edge as the years go by.
