Getting serious about tax optimization, Bitcoin DCA, portfolio tracking

If you are just starting out, it is tempting to just buy whenever you feel like it. But that is how you end up with a tax nightmare. By sticking to a strict schedule, I’ve found that it is much easier to keep a clean record of my cost basis. This is where automating my DCA buys saved my life. Instead of making random manual trades that create a million tiny line items, I have a predictable stream of transactions that are easy to export and categorize.

I know some people argue that you should just keep buying manually to "time the market," but I honestly think that is a trap. The hidden cost of manual management

The biggest mistake I made early on was using too many different exchanges. I thought I was being clever by spreading my risk, but I was really just creating a massive headache for myself. Every time I had to pull a tax report, I had to deal with different formats, different API limitations, and different fee structures. Now, I try to keep it simple. If you are looking for a reliable place to start, I’ve had the best luck when I buy Bitcoin on Coinmate. It’s straightforward and plays nice with the automated tools I use.

Tracking your progress is just as important as the buying part. I have different buckets for my goals—a retirement fund, a "house" fund, and a small emergency fund. I use the features of my DCA tool to see how each of those buckets is performing against my initial projections. It keeps me from getting discouraged when the price dips, because I can see that I am still hitting my accumulation targets regardless of the short-term volatility.
